01 // THE PITCH (WHAT, WHO, WHY)

A Slot Machine Where Winning Takes a Piece of You

Set in a world where gambling is omnipresent, an antagonist who lost everything through addiction has trapped gambling addicts in a chair. You are strapped in at arm's reach of a brutalist slot machine and must pull the lever repeatedly. The twist: hitting the jackpot is BAD. Every non-jackpot pull unlocks 1 of 6 shackles. Every jackpot triggers the backlit antagonist to enter, take a body part (implied via cut-to-black and clinical sound design—zero explicit gore), and hand you strategy powerup cards in exchange.

Who it's for & why play it: Built for fans of tense tabletop-style psychological horror and probability strategy games (like Buckshot Roulette and Inscryption). Instead of chasing a dopamine jackpot, you are actively trying to rig the machine against yourself so you can survive 6 clean pulls and escape.

02 // FIRST SESSION (FIRST FEW MINUTES)

In the first 3 minutes, the machine powers on with a diegetic 40–45% jackpot probability etched onto its face. You pull the lever, hear the 3 independent reel cylinders clunk to a stop, and either unlock your first shackle or face your first sacrifice. If a jackpot lands, you must choose between safe medical Stitching (no bonus) or risking a Second Body Part for a top-tier odds-rigging card.

WHAT BRINGS THEM BACK TOMORROW

Even after all 6 shackles unlock, the player is given a final psychological test: walk out the door, or choose to stay in the chair and keep spinning. That high-stakes greed hook—paired with discovering how the 5 powerup cards and internal wiring dials interact—drives immediate "one more run" retention.

03 // PROGRESSION & MONTHS-LONG METAGAME

Day-1 Greed Hook & Multi-Month Depth

The Day-1 Hook Built: Two interlocking hooks drive immediate replayability on Day 1. First is the Sacrifice Risk/Reward Dilemma—choosing whether to accept safe medical stitching after a jackpot or sacrifice a second body part (suffering heavier screen distortion and losing card tray slots) to draw from the top-tier card pool. Second is the Post-Escape Greed Hook: once all 6 shackles are unlocked, you don't have to leave—you can choose to stay in the chair and keep spinning to test how far your rigged deck can push your luck.

What Keeps Someone Playing for Months: Expanding the 5-card prototype into a deep roguelike deckbuilder. Using the Pact of Punishment ("Heat" 0–57) structure I analyzed from Hades in my Obsidian vault, players who escape unlock higher-stakes interrogation tables, new antagonist dealers with unique rigging rules, 40+ unlockable cards, and complex back-panel wiring puzzles.

04 // MONEY (WITHOUT FEELING LIKE A CASH GRAB)

Monetizing an Anti-Gambling Thriller Ethically

Because Spin to Lose is a psychological critique of gambling addiction, selling real-money revives or pay-to-win probability boosts would completely destroy the game's tension and thematic integrity.

  • Diegetic Machine & Card Cosmetics: Players purchase custom retro CRT/brutalist slot machine chassis skins, custom Aseprite pixel-art card backs, and custom shackle/tray themes that sit physically in the 3D room.
  • DLC Antagonist Acts & Expansion Decks: The base 6-shackle survival mode and core card pool are 100% free. Players pay a flat price ($2.99–$4.99) for new seasonal "Acts" featuring new antagonists, new mechanical contraptions, and new 15-card strategy archetypes.
  • Zero Forced Ads: Interrupting a horror atmosphere with a banner or mid-run video ad kills immersion. Any ad integration is strictly opt-in between runs in the lobby.
05 // AI: HOW WE BUILT IT & IN-GAME AI

Engineering Acceleration + Adaptive Dealer AI

How AI Was Used to Build It: Built during the Juniper Devs Game Jam under a strict No AI Art or Audio rule, every 3D mesh (Blender low-poly flat-shaded models), pixel-art texture (Aseprite reel symbols and cards), and foley sound (Audacity + LMMS) was 100% hand-made by our 3-person team. On the code side, I used AI coding collaborators (Claude Code / Gemini) to accelerate writing the custom ~20-line HLSL fullscreen posterization URP Renderer Feature shader, C# reel cylinder state machines, and Inspector-exposed AudioClip/GameObject fields so my 3D and Audio teammates could wire assets without blocking on code.

AI Inside the Game Itself: The Antagonist operates as an Adaptive Psychological Dealer AI. Rather than pulling from a purely static table, the dealer tracks player risk patterns (whether you play conservatively with Stitching or greedily sacrifice extra body parts, and how heavily you stack odds-lowering cards) to dynamically select voice lines, alter loot-tier offerings, and pressure your specific survival strategy.

06 & 07 // SHIPPING METRICS, KILL TRIGGERS & REFERENCES

What We Test First, Soft Launch NSMs & Reference Games

What We Test First & Kill/Pivot Triggers: The #1 variable to test is the 40–45% starting jackpot probability and the 8–15 minute target run length. If players die on Pull 2 before drawing a single card, they churn from perceived unfairness; if they unlock all 6 shackles without ever hitting a jackpot, they miss the central sacrifice mechanic. Following our Scope Safety Net, if playtesters find the back-panel Open the Machine wiring tool confusing, we cut it first and focus 100% on the 5-card tray loop.

Numbers Watched at Soft Launch (LILA NSM Framework): Focus on D1 Retention (target >40%) and Average Core Loop Actions (8+ runs/day) at Soft Launch, then shift serially to Organic ARPDAU ($0.15) and D7 ROAS (20%) at Hard Launch.

Reference Games Pulled Apart: Buckshot Roulette (borrowed the 4–6 band posterization shader, no anti-aliasing, harsh overhead point light, fixed first-person POV at arm's reach, and backlit antagonist silhouette) and Inscryption (diegetic in-world card tray and body-part sacrifice). What we did differently: Inverted the slot machine fantasy—turning the jackpot from a reward into a psychological horror penalty where you use strategy cards to rig the machine to lose.

SECTION 02 // QUESTION #2 DELIVERABLE

Gameplay Insights: Interactive Narrative F2P

SUB-GENRE: STORY MODE GAMES // REINVENTION BLUEPRINT
EDITORIAL OP-ED // WHY STORY MODE DISAPPEARED

The Top 100 App Store "Slop" Problem & The Telltale Vacuum

Recently I browsed the top 100 games in the App Store just to see what was on there, and all of it was just slop. All the games were fast-paced, high-dopamine games. As a model, it's fine to have games whose entire design is just to capture the user's attention for as long as possible, but it takes the "fun" part about games in my opinion. There was a time where the App Store had good story-defined games which were actually fun and playable, that wanted more from the people who were playing it. You needed to take notes about the objectives and not have the game feed it to you.

Popular games from the Telltale series like The Walking Dead, Batman, Borderlands, etc., had an interactive story-based method, where the user's action would deeply impact the story. You needed to listen to the story as it progressed to be able to understand what was going on and influence it, but now I don't see any games that represent even 1% of what Telltale Games produced for the F2P market. It was only one ep which was free at first, but that one ep hooked so many people every single game they did. To change that, a few changes can be done to the innate design of the tailored story mode games.

TWEAK 01 // 2-MINUTE EDGE OF SEAT

Pacing & Quick-Time Events

We need to take into account the short attention span of people and add tweaks to the story based on that. Every 2 minutes we need to keep the viewer on the edge of the seat as he witnesses the story being played out. The more filler scenes which have more white spaces, we remove them from the story and make the calm story-like game into an interactive one which thrives with the viewer's attention.

Keeping the story the same (like Telltale's The Walking Dead) but adding more quick-time events and reducing the gap time between each dialogue: if Lee gets to choose from 4 options, instead of then waiting again for like 5 mins for another set to come up, cut that in half. It adds more that the player can do for the game rather than just staring at a screen and watching that cutscene happen.

TWEAK 02 // TAILORED BY THE PLAYER

Environmental Interactivity

This format isn't particularly following the open-world concept as it's mainly guided by the story, and having only certain areas explorable. Instead of increasing the explorable area, we add more interactables for the main character.

The game is very linear as you can only interact with a few things and talk to people, that's all. But if you were to add more interactivity for the character, then it would be more fun. E.g., adding more choices of weapons in the game which you can select from, which would make the story more tailored "by the player".

FIG. 2.3 // PRODUCTION SCOPE MATRIX

Lesser Budget vs. Expensive Side

Now, some of these can be expensive to the production to make. Adding more events and more choices can lead to the devs making more different scenarios, which would lead to adding more budget to the game, which can't really be feasible:

FOR A LESSER BUDGET:

We do the interactivity part of the environment, as it's just adding a few more actions that the player can do to the environment (such as weapon selection in existing rooms).

FOR THE EXPENSIVE SIDE:

We do the quick-time events which tailor the story more to the player and having multiple endings and dialogues.

F2P ECONOMY SPECIFICATION

Subtle 10/20 Energy System, Dual-Currency & Zero Forced Ads

To monetize an interactive narrative game effectively without ruining the story, we need to implement a subtle energy system. For example, starting an episode might cost 10 energy bars out of a maximum capacity of 20. Once depleted, players would need to replenish their energy either by waiting it out over time or by purchasing more from the in-game store.

This economy would rely on a dual-currency system: energy bars and a premium in-game currency (e.g., "dollars"). Energy replenishes naturally, but if a player runs out, they can buy premium dollars to purchase immediate energy refills. To maintain a fair experience for strictly F2P players who don't want to spend real money, completing an episode would reward them with a small amount of premium dollars, giving them a path to earn convenience items for free.

Crucially, there should be absolutely no forced ads during the episodes. Interrupting the story with ads completely ruins the user's immersion and interactivity. Instead, ads should be strictly opt-in, where players can choose to watch a rewarded video ad exclusively in exchange for a free energy bar.

SECTION 03 // QUESTION #3 DELIVERABLE

Design Spec: Roguelike Build & Elemental System

SCENARIO: MOBILE SURVIVOR-LIKE (SURVIVOR.IO / VAMPIRE SURVIVORS)
FEATURE RATIONALE & CORE MECHANICS

Why Survivor.io Needs True Roguelike Build Randomness

Let's take Survivor.io as an example and to add a new interactive feature to it, I would honestly go for a roguelike-ish combat system, it still being fully automated but every run is new for the player. He would need to have a starter weapon and he'll need a build for that, so he starts off with a certain ability and we would have it so that none of the builds can ever be the same, adding that very randomness every time. It would engage the player a lot, as a survival game can get boring pretty fast, and you're not even doing the combat—you're just dodging and running. Adding a build system would be very beneficial.

If we were to go for a throwable build, we would select our starter weapons, but that only affects the nature of our primary weapon now. Kunais would lead to just a throwing base attack, but the thing about roguelikes is, your main attack can also not be your main build. We could get another overpowered missile cast that doubles after every hit and stops for 1 sec as a cooldown, and you can also go with just a high-DPS single-strike throwable build—the possibilities are endless with the build system. Implementing a roguelike system of elements on the other hand would lead the characters to even be more versatile with their build: Kunai + Water as a starter weapon, you get a silent yet deadly build and you can keep upgrading other things.
